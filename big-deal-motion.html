01 · TALENT SCARCITY

Growth can stall when critical talent is harder to secure than capital or demand.

Discovery

“If your growth plans over the next 2–3 years succeed, where could talent availability become the biggest constraint to execution?”

How is leadership measuring the business impact of those shortages?
Which business priorities would be most affected if hiring slowed?
Who beyond HR feels the impact most directly?
Outreach narrative

Talent as growth infrastructure

Many companies already have capital, technology and market demand. The differentiator is whether critical talent can be secured ahead of competitors.

Executive opening
Could talent availability become the hidden constraint in your growth plan?
Executive one-pager

Can we secure the talent needed to deliver our growth ambition?

LinkedIn Hiring Enterprise
Reach and engage priority professional talent pools.
LIHA
Support recruiter productivity and broader candidate discovery.
LCP
Build awareness and preference among target talent audiences.
Business outcome
Greater talent visibility, recruiter leverage and confidence in the hiring plan.
Illustrative customer story · adapt before external use

Semiconductor expansion

A Korean semiconductor supplier secures new contracts and needs to rapidly expand engineering capability. Leadership initially assumes the answer is more recruiter capacity.

The account team reframes the issue: scarce talent is concentrated, passive candidates have low awareness of the employer, and the existing network is too narrow.

Executive question

“Are we short on recruiters, or are we invisible to the talent we need?”

Suggested value path

Map target pools with enterprise hiring, build relevant employer visibility through LCP, and use LIHA selectively to add discovery coverage and recruiter leverage.

Illustrative customer story · adapt before external use

AI transformation

A Korean technology company launches an enterprise AI strategy. Funding is available and the roadmap is approved, yet execution begins to slow.

Leadership discovers that technology access is not the only constraint. Competition for AI engineers, data specialists and technical product leaders is affecting delivery confidence.

Executive question

“What happens if competitors secure the AI talent before we do?”

Suggested value path

Identify addressable pools, sharpen the AI employer story, and create recruiter leverage without positioning automation as a replacement for precision sourcing.

Illustrative customer story · adapt before external use

Korea to global

A Korean company expands into Southeast Asia and North America. Referral networks and familiar domestic channels have worked well at home but do not translate consistently abroad.

The company is credible in Korea, yet target candidates in new markets have limited awareness of its story, opportunities and leadership ambition.

Executive question

“How do we compete for talent when the people we need do not yet know who we are?”

Suggested value path

Combine global professional reach, market-aware employer storytelling and recruiting leverage around the priority expansion roles.

Korea executive objection playbook

Most objections are requests for confidence.

Treat the concern as valid. Ask permission to understand it. Avoid forcing the buyer to contradict a senior stakeholder or defend a cautious position.

1 · Validate
“I understand why that would be a concern.”
2 · Ask permission
“May I ask one question to understand it better?”
3 · Explore privately
Clarify the decision process without challenging authority.
“I don't have budget.”

It may signal: low priority, unclear ownership, or an internal business case that is not yet strong enough.

May I understand how investments like this are typically evaluated internally?
When leadership prioritizes initiatives, which outcomes tend to receive the most support?
If this became more important to the business, whose perspective would be helpful to include?
Respectful reframe
Perhaps the question is not whether budget exists today, but whether the business impact is significant enough to justify investment.
“It is too expensive for something that is not localized.”

It may signal: adoption risk, ROI concern, preference for familiar local solutions, or reluctance to sponsor a visible risk.

Which aspects of localization are most important for your team and users today?
If the business outcome were strong, which localization gaps would still create the greatest concern?
Would it be fair to explore whether the main concern is localization itself or confidence in the value?
Respectful reframe
The question may not be perfect localization. It may be whether the solution addresses a business challenge that remains unresolved today.
“I don't want to commit long term.”

It may signal: limited confidence, unclear success measures, incomplete internal alignment, or perceived implementation risk.

May I ask what would help increase confidence in a longer-term decision?
If we reviewed progress six months from now, what would you need to see to feel the decision was working?
Which risks would be most important to address before considering a broader partnership?
Respectful reframe
It sounds as though the issue may not be commitment itself. It may be confidence in the path and the measures of success.

Bigger Deal Hour

One account. One theme. One experiment.

Choose

Pick one must-win account and the theme with the strongest business evidence.

Prepare

Customize one discovery question, one outreach narrative and one executive hypothesis.

Test

Run the smallest useful experiment: a new question, a new stakeholder or a new approval aid.

Learn

What did we try? What did we learn? What is the next move?
